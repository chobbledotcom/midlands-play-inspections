      ## A tag on the unit, a report in your inbox

      Every unit that passes gets a **PIPA tag** fitted, which is the closest thing an
      inflatable has to a number plate. Anyone can look that tag up on the PIPA
      database and see a live inspection status, which is what a school, a
      council or a venue booking officer will do.

      Alongside it you get a **PDF inspection report**: the unit identified by
      make, serial and size, every check point recorded, photographs of anything
      of note, and a plain-English summary. It's written to be forwarded, so send
      it straight to your insurer or a venue without having to explain it.

      Units that fall outside the PIPA scheme get the equivalent EN 14960 or
      bespoke inspection and a report in the same format, just without a tag.

      ## A fail isn't the end of the day

      Most failures are small: a worn anchor patch, a split seam, a missing
      blower guard, a net that's gone brittle. You'll be told what it is, why
      it matters and what will fix it, rather than just handed a red mark.

      Where a repair is straightforward and can be done on the spot, we'll say
      so. Where it needs the manufacturer or a specialist repairer, we'll point
      you at one. Once the fix is done, the unit is re-checked and tagged,
      usually without a second full inspection fee.

      Nothing gets tagged that shouldn't be. That's the whole point of the tag
      being worth something.
