      - question: Do bouncy castles legally have to be tested?
        answer: >-
          If you hire them out commercially, yes. Health and safety law requires

          equipment offered to the public for hire or reward to have in-service

          safety inspections, and HSE guidance expects amusement devices to be

          checked **annually by a competent person**.


          There isn't a single line of statute that says "bouncy castles must be

          PIPA tested". The duty comes from the Health and Safety at Work Act
          and

          PUWER, with the detail set out in HSE's guidance, HSG175. PIPA is one
          of

          two inspection schemes named in that guidance, which is why it's the

          proof most venues ask for.
      - question: What's the difference between PIPA and RPII?
        answer: >-
          **RPII**, the Register of Play Inspectors International, registers the

          *person*. It's how you check that an inspector is qualified and
          current.


          **PIPA** is the *scheme* for inflatable play specifically. It sets the

          inspection framework, runs the tag database, and holds inspectors to
          its

          rules. A PIPA inspector is RPII registered; not everything an RPII

          inspector tests carries a PIPA tag.


          In short: RPII says who we are, PIPA says what your unit has been

          through.
      - question: What is BS EN 14960?
        answer: >-
          BS EN 14960-1:2019 is the British and European standard for inflatable

          play equipment, covering safety requirements and test methods. It's
          what sets the

          numbers we measure against: wall heights, anchor point strength,
          netting

          mesh sizes, entrapment gaps, operating pressures and so on.


          When you see "tested to EN 14960" on a report, that's the yardstick.
      - question: Am I covered if I only use my castle at home?
        answer: >-
          A privately owned inflatable used only by your own family isn't hired
          out

          for reward, so the commercial duty doesn't apply. The moment you lend
          it,

          hire it out, or use it at a fete or a club event, you're in different

          territory, and your insurer will very likely want to see a current

          inspection.


          If in doubt, get it tested. It's cheaper than the alternative.

      - question: How often does an inflatable need inspecting?
        answer: >-
          Every twelve months. The inspection remains valid for 12 months from
          the inspection date and must be renewed before it expires.


          Book slightly early rather than slightly late, because a tag that
          expires

          mid-season is a booking you can't take.
      - question: How long does an inspection take?
        answer: >-
          A standard children's castle takes around 30 minutes to an hour.
          Larger  

          units, obstacle courses and anything with a lot of netting take
          longer.  

          On a fleet day we'll usually get through eight to twelve units
          depending  

          on size and how the store is laid out.


          Having units accessible and roughly in order makes a real difference
          to

          how many we can do.
      - question: When do I get the report?
        answer: Within a few days, by email, as a PDF. You're covered for bookings
          immediately, and the report is the paperwork that follows.
      - question: How does someone check my test?
        answer: You’ll receive a PDF inspection report that you can send by email to
          customers, venues or insurers. If you use a BCN website, you can also
          upload it through the back end so a visible test certificate appears
          on your website.
      - question: Do you inspect in winter?
        answer: >-
          Winter is the best time to do it. Your equipment isn't earning, we've
          got

          more availability, and you start the season with everything in date.
          We

          need a dry indoor space big enough to inflate the unit, so a village
          hall,

          a barn or a decent-sized unit all work.

      ## Tags, reports and timing

  - type: faqs
    dark: false
    compact: false
    items:
      - question: What should I ask a hire company to show me?
        answer: >-
          Three things: a **current inspection report**, the unit’s identifying
          details and the operator’s **public liability insurance**.


          Check that the report is still valid and that the serial number and
          dimensions match the inflatable that has arrived.
      - question: A hirer says their unit doesn't need a tag. Is that right?
        answer: >-
          Yes. A PIPA tag is not a legal requirement. What is required is a
          current annual safety inspection carried out by a competent person to
          the applicable requirements of BS EN 14960. Ask to see the inspection
          report and check that it matches the inflatable supplied.


          "It doesn't need testing" with nothing to show you is a different
          answer,

          and not a good one.
      - question: Can you inspect equipment we own ourselves?
        answer: >-
          Yes. Schools, nurseries, holiday parks, soft play centres and councils
          all

          book us directly for equipment they own rather than hire. Same
          inspection,

          same report, and it goes straight into your health and safety file.

      ## For schools, councils and venues

  - type: faqs
    dark: false
    compact: false
    items:
      - question: Can you test a unit imported from overseas?
        answer: >-
          Usually, but go in expecting some remedial work. Plenty of imported

          inflatables are built to something the manufacturer has understood as
          EN

          14960 rather than to the standard itself, and the things that most
          often

          come up are wall heights, anchor point strength and the size of the
          gaps

          round netting and arches.


          None of that is a reason not to buy abroad. It is a reason to get an

          initial inspection done before the unit takes a booking, so you find
          out

          on your own time rather than a week before a school fete.
      - question: Do you do repairs?
        answer: >-
          We inspect, we don't currently repair. If something fails we'll tell
          you exactly what  

          it is, why it matters and what will put it right, and we'll point you
          at a  

          repairer who can do that particular job properly.


          Once the work is done we re-check the unit and tag it, usually without
          a

          second full inspection fee. The reason we don't do both is that an

          inspector who repairs the thing he has just failed is marking his own

          homework, and we would rather not be in that position.
      - question: What about sealed air inflatables?
        answer: >-
          They still need inspecting if you hire them out,  they sit outside
          the  

          PIPA scheme a RPII test is better here, so they get a bespoke
          inspection rather than a tag. Sealed  

          units are a specialist item and the inspection looks at the design and
          the  

          maintenance history as well as the condition, so send us the make,
          model  

          and any paperwork you have before booking.
      - question: My unit came without an operating manual. Does that matter?
        answer: >-
          It matters more than most people expect. The manufacturer's operating

          instructions are what set the anchor point count, the operating
          pressure

          and the user limits for that specific unit, and without them there is

          nothing to inspect against.


          If the manufacturer has gone, or the unit came secondhand with
          nothing,

          an operations manual can be put together for it. Sort that out before
          an

          inspection rather than finding out on the day.
      - question: How do I check you're actually qualified?
        answer: >-
          Ask for the registration number and look it up yourself. RPII
          registration

          is checkable at playinspectors.com and PIPA inspectors are listed on
          the

          PIPA site. Do it for us and do it for anybody else quoting you.


          The same goes the other way round. If a venue asks you to prove your
          units  

          are inspected, give them the tag numbers and test PDFs without fuss.
          That is what they are for.
