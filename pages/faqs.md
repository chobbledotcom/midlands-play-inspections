      ## The law and the schemes
    items:
      - question: Do bouncy castles legally have to be tested?
        answer: |
          If you hire them out commercially, yes. Health and safety law requires
          equipment offered to the public for hire or reward to have in-service
          safety inspections, and HSE guidance expects amusement devices to be
          checked **annually by a competent person**.

          There isn't a single line of statute that says "bouncy castles must be
          PIPA tested". The duty comes from the Health and Safety at Work Act and
          PUWER, with the detail set out in HSE's guidance, HSG175. PIPA is one of
          two inspection schemes named in that guidance, which is why it's the
          proof most venues ask for.
      - question: What's the difference between PIPA and RPII?
        answer: |
          **RPII**, the Register of Play Inspectors International, registers the
          *person*. It's how you check that an inspector is qualified and current.

          **PIPA** is the *scheme* for inflatable play specifically. It sets the
          inspection framework, runs the tag database, and holds inspectors to its
          rules. A PIPA inspector is RPII registered; not everything an RPII
          inspector tests carries a PIPA tag.

          In short: RPII says who we are, PIPA says what your unit has been
          through.
      - question: What is BS EN 14960?
        answer: |
          BS EN 14960-1:2019 is the British and European standard for inflatable
          play equipment, covering safety requirements and test methods. It's what sets the
          numbers we measure against: wall heights, anchor point strength, netting
          mesh sizes, entrapment gaps, operating pressures and so on.

          When you see "tested to EN 14960" on a report, that's the yardstick.
      - question: Am I covered if I only use my castle at home?
        answer: |
          A privately owned inflatable used only by your own family isn't hired out
          for reward, so the commercial duty doesn't apply. The moment you lend it,
          hire it out, or use it at a fete or a club event, you're in different
          territory, and your insurer will very likely want to see a current
          inspection.

          If in doubt, get it tested. It's cheaper than the alternative.

      ## Tags, reports and timing
    items:
      - question: How often does an inflatable need inspecting?
        answer: |
          Every twelve months. A PIPA tag runs for a year from the inspection date,
          and the database will show it as out of date the moment it lapses.

          Book slightly early rather than slightly late, because a tag that expires
          mid-season is a booking you can't take.
      - question: How long does an inspection take?
        answer: |
          A standard children's castle takes around 45 minutes to an hour. Larger
          units, obstacle courses and anything with a lot of netting take longer.
          On a fleet day we'll usually get through eight to twelve units depending
          on size and how the store is laid out.

          Having units accessible and roughly in order makes a real difference to
          how many we can do.
      - question: When do I get the report?
        answer: |
          Within a few days, by email, as a PDF. The tag goes on the unit before we
          leave, so you're covered for bookings immediately, and the report is the
          paperwork that follows.
      - question: How does someone check my tag?
        answer: |
          Anyone can look a PIPA tag up on the PIPA website and see its current
          status and inspection report. That's the point of it. A venue booking officer
          or a school business manager can verify your unit in about fifteen
          seconds without ringing anyone.
      - question: Do you inspect in winter?
        answer: |
          Winter is the best time to do it. Your equipment isn't earning, we've got
          more availability, and you start the season with everything in date. We
          need a dry indoor space big enough to inflate the unit, so a village hall,
          a barn or a decent-sized unit all work.

      ## For schools, councils and venues
    items:
      - question: What should I ask a hire company to show me?
        answer: |
          Three things: a **current PIPA tag** on the unit itself, the matching
          **inspection report**, and their **public liability insurance**. Check the
          tag number against the PIPA database rather than taking a photo of a
          certificate at face value.

          Also check the report actually matches the unit that turned up, since serial
          numbers and dimensions are on both.
      - question: A hirer says their unit doesn't need a tag. Is that right?
        answer: |
          Sometimes, genuinely, because some equipment sits outside the PIPA scheme's
          scope and gets an EN 14960 or bespoke inspection instead. That's fine, but
          they should still be able to hand you a current inspection report from a
          registered inspector.

          "It doesn't need testing" with nothing to show you is a different answer,
          and not a good one.
      - question: Can you inspect equipment we own ourselves?
        answer: |
          Yes. Schools, nurseries, holiday parks, soft play centres and councils all
          book us directly for equipment they own rather than hire. Same inspection,
          same report, and it goes straight into your health and safety file.
