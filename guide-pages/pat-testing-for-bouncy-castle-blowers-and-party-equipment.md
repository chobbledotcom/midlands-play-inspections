      Every bouncy castle runs on a blower, and every blower runs on mains
      electricity, usually outdoors, on damp grass, with children close by.
      That's why the blower gets checked as closely as the castle.


      The short answer: PAT testing itself isn't a legal requirement, but
      keeping your electrical equipment safe is. A PAT test is the most common
      way of showing you've done that.

      ## Is PAT testing a legal requirement?


      Not as such. The Electricity at Work Regulations 1989 require electrical
      equipment that could cause injury to be kept in a safe condition. HSE is
      clear that the law doesn't say how that has to be done, by whom, or how
      often, and that there's no legal requirement to test every year.


      What it does mean is that you need some way of keeping your kit safe, and
      of showing you've done it. For hire equipment, HSE's guidance says the
      supplier should inspect and test it before each hire, and the person
      hiring it should make sure it stays safe while they have it.


      For a hire operator, that usually means a careful check of the blower and
      lead every time they go out, backed up by a full PAT test at regular
      intervals. When a venue or school asks to see that your electrics are
      safe, a PAT test record is what they're expecting.

    dark: false

      ## The three levels of checking


      HSE describes three levels, and each one catches different problems:


      - **User checks.** A quick look before every use: plug, cable, casing and
      connections. Anyone using the kit can do this, and it catches most damage.

      - **Formal visual inspection.** A closer, recorded check by someone
      trained to do it, including taking the plug top off to look inside.

      - **Combined inspection and testing.** The full PAT test, carried out by a
      competent person with proper test equipment. This finds faults you can't
      see, such as a broken earth or breaking-down insulation.


      HSE points out that a visual check is essential, because some faults can't
      be found by testing. A good PAT test always starts with a proper look.

      ## How often should a blower be PAT tested?


      There's no fixed interval in law. HSE and the IET's Code of Practice both
      say to set the interval by risk: how the equipment is used, where, and how
      hard it works.


      A bouncy castle blower is at the hard-working end of that. It's carried in
      and out of vans, used outdoors, run for hours, and plugged into whatever
      supply the venue has. It needs checking more often than, say, a desk lamp
      in an office.


      At a minimum, have your blowers and leads PAT tested every year, and do a
      user check every time they go out. If a blower has been dropped, got wet
      or tripped a supply, check it before it's used again.

      ## What a PAT test covers on a blower


      - **Visual inspection:** the plug, fuse rating, cable grip, flex, casing,
      switch and the guard over the fan.

      - **Earth continuity:** for earthed (Class I) equipment, checking the
      earth path is sound.

      - **Insulation resistance:** checking the insulation hasn't broken down.

      - **Leads:** fly leads and extension leads get the same checks, plus a
      polarity check to make sure they're wired correctly.


      Anything that fails is written up with what's wrong and what will put it
      right, the same as a fault on the castle.
  - type: markdown

      ## Extension leads and cable reels


      Leads get forgotten, but they take as much punishment as the blower. Check
      them as carefully, and include them in your PAT testing.


      When you run a blower off a cable reel, unwind it fully. A reel left
      partly wound can overheat under load.

      ## Labels and records


      HSE says there's no legal requirement to label tested equipment or keep
      records. Both are still worth doing. A sticker lets anyone see at a glance
      that an item has been tested and when, and a record shows you have a
      system in place. It's what you'll be asked for if anything goes wrong.
  - type: markdown
    dark: false
    compact: false
    content: >-
      ## Does new equipment need a PAT test?


      No. HSE says new equipment should be supplied safe and doesn't need a
      formal PAT test before it's used. Give it a visual check to make sure it
      hasn't been damaged in transit, and bring it into your normal testing
      after that.

      ## Daily blower checks


      Alongside the electrics, HSE expects you to check the blower every day
      it's used:


      - the fan blades are clear of grass and debris

      - the non-return flap on the outlet works properly

      - the connection tube and blower are firmly attached to each other

      - you're using the right blower for that unit, as set out in the manual
