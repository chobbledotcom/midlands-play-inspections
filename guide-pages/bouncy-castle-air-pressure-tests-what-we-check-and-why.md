      ## Why does air pressure matter?


      ### Helping prevent bottoming out


      Bottoming out happens when a user compresses the inflatable bed enough to
      hit the ground beneath it. A castle may look inflated but still have
      insufficient pressure to cushion users properly.


      The pressure inside a bouncy castle or any inflatable where users will be
      on or under the equipment must be above 1kpa. Apart form a couple of
      inflatable types, like air mountains.


      ### Supporting the walls and barriers


      Internal pressure helps the inflatable bed, walls and retaining barriers
      maintain their intended shape and firmness.


      Low pressure can allow users to fall out of the inflatable or pull down
      the walls on to other users.

      ## How do we measure the pressure?


      We use a manometer, an instrument designed to measure pressure. We insert
      the tube inside the inflatable, normally by the zip area.


      ## What pressure should a bouncy castle have?


      Always check the manufacturer’s operating manual for the pressure
      requirements of the particular inflatable. HSE says an inflatable should
      not be used below the manufacturer’s minimum pressure or above its
      specified maximum.


      You may see 1 kPa, mentioned in discussions about inflatable testing. That
      figure should not be treated as a universal pass mark for every
      inflatable.


      The correct reading must be considered alongside the inflatable’s design,
      operating instructions and applicable inspection requirements.

      ## A real air pressure test


      During a recent inspection, we measured 1.3 kPa inside an adult bouncy
      castle. For that particular castle, the minimum pressure we were checking
      against was 1 kPa, so it passed the pressure check.


      ## What can cause low pressure?


      A low reading can prompt further investigation into the inflatable and its
      blower setup. Possible causes include:


      - Air escaping through damaged fabric or seams.

      - Deflation openings that have not been properly closed.

      - A loose or poorly secured blower connection.

      - An obstructed inflation tube.

      - An unsuitable or poorly performing blower.


      The pressure reading helps identify a problem, but it does not diagnose
      the cause on its own.

      ## Should pressure be checked between annual inspections?


      Yes a manometer should be used when an inflatable is set up. If suitable
      measuring equipment is unavailable, the inflatable should not be operated.


      An annual inspection records the condition at the time of testing.
      Operators still need to check the equipment during everyday use.
