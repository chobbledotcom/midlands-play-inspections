    content: |-
      The short answer is that a tag isn't the requirement. If you hire
      inflatables out for money, health and safety law requires the equipment to
      have an in-service safety inspection every twelve months, carried out by a
      competent person. That duty comes from the Health and Safety at Work Act
      and PUWER, with the detail set out in HSE's guidance, HSG175. There isn't
      a line of statute anywhere that says a bouncy castle must carry a PIPA
      tag.

      What a tag does is prove it. A PIPA tag is fitted after an inspection to
      BS EN 14960-1:2019, it carries a number, and that number can be checked on
      the public PIPA database against the unit's serial and its inspection
      date. When a venue, a school or a council asks whether your castle is
      tested, the tag is the answer they can verify themselves without ringing
      you.
  - type: markdown
    content: |-
      ## What to have ready when somebody asks

      Three things cover almost every request. A current inspection report, the
      unit's identifying details, and the tag number to check against the
      database. The report should still be in date, and the serial number and
      dimensions on it should match the inflatable that actually turned up on
      the day. Most venues want the report emailed over before the booking
      rather than produced from the van, so keeping the PDFs on your phone is
      worth the bother.

      If a hirer tells you their unit doesn't need a tag, they are right, up to
      a point. What is required is a current annual inspection by a competent
      person to the applicable requirements of BS EN 14960. "It doesn't need a
      tag" is a fair answer when there is a report to go with it. The same words
      with nothing to show you is a different answer.
