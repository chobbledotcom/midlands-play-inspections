      The short answer is that a tag isn't a requirement. If you hire
      inflatables out for money, health and safety law requires the equipment to
      have a test every twelve months, carried out by a competent person to BS
      EN 14960. That duty comes from the Health and Safety at Work Act and
      PUWER, with the detail set out in HSE's guidance, HSG175. There isn't a
      line of statute anywhere that says a bouncy castle must carry a PIPA tag.


      What a tag does is help prove it. A PIPA tag is fitted after an inspection
      to BS EN 14960-1:2019, it carries a number, and that number can be checked
      on the public PIPA database against the unit's serial and its inspection
      date.


      However a test report sent via email dose the same.

      ## What to have ready when somebody asks


      Three things cover almost every request. A current inspection report, the
      unit's identifying details, and the tag number or test report to check
      against the database. The report should still be in date, and the serial
      number, image and dimensions on it should match the inflatable that
      actually turned up on the day. Most venues want the report emailed over
      before the booking rather than produced from the van, so keeping the PDFs
      on your phone is worth the bother.


      If a hirer tells you their unit doesn't need a tag, they are right. What
      is required is a current annual inspection by a competent person to the
      applicable requirements of BS EN 14960. "It doesn't need a tag" is a fair
      answer when there is a report to go with it. The same words with nothing
      to show you is a different answer.
